# Aerchain product teardown

**Prashant Kumar** · Product Manager · [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/prashant-kumar-0b069180/)

A 65-part, evidence-based teardown of [Aerchain](https://aerchain.io/), an AI procurement platform for large enterprises. It covers what the product does, why enterprises pay for it, why they stay or leave, where it is defensible and vulnerable, and what I would do as its PM.

**[Read the full teardown →](teardown.md)**

## The short version

Aerchain sells AI software that runs a large company's buying process, from an employee's request to the supplier's invoice. Each step is handled by an AI agent, meaning software that takes actions and does not only answer questions, and the customer decides how much each agent may do alone. The product idea is strong and the customer names are impressive for a company this size. Its weakest point is proof: value and reliability are asserted more than they are measured, and every large rival shipped procurement agents in 2026.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core problem it solves** | Procurement teams get more purchase requests than they can handle, the work runs on email and spreadsheets, and older procurement suites take over a year to install and are avoided by employees. |
| **Strongest feature** | Reading supplier bids in any format into one comparison, with suppliers able to answer by email without a login. |
| **Weakest feature** | Analytics, which is the feature that proves value to the finance team. |
| **Biggest threat** | Established suites and finance-system vendors bundling good-enough agents into contracts customers already hold. |
| **North Star Metric I'd use** | Value of purchases completed end to end through Aerchain each month. |
| **What I'd do first as PM** | Build the evidence layer: a value record the customer's finance team signs off, a scorecard that shows when a spending category is ready for agent autonomy, and testing that makes agents safe to change. |

## How it was built

- **Every important claim is labelled** FACT (a source says it directly), INFERENCE (my reasoned conclusion) or HYPOTHESIS (plausible, but not confirmable from public information). Where a number isn't public, the document says so instead of guessing.
- **Estimates show their working.** Aerchain publishes no pricing, retention or unit-economics figures, so every such number is an estimate with the assumptions and arithmetic written out.
- **Conflicting company figures are shown side by side.** Some of Aerchain's public numbers differ between its own pages and press coverage. The teardown lists each version with its source instead of picking one.
- **Sources are listed** in a register at the end, rated by reliability.
- **Public information only.** I haven't used the product. Aerchain has no free trial or public documentation, so the workflow and UX analysis is built from its product pages, press coverage, job postings and reviews.
- **AI-assisted research.** I wrote a 65-part research brief and used Claude to research and draft against it. I then reviewed and edited the result.
